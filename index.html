Act 01Decoding Geometry & Anytime Control

Cut the token stream mid-flight—and watch five codecs break differently.

Zero-parameter spectral and geometric codecs already beat 1,300-GPU-hour neural autoencoders at every bitrate.1,4,6 What decides policy quality now is how a codec degrades under budget cuts and single-token errors: halving Cover (EEF) mid-stream (50.3 → 25.2 tok, 1.85× faster) keeps 76% vs. 84% SR, while halving OAT (16 → 8 tok, 19 ms) holds 80%.7,8,9,10

Act 04Exp 4 & Exp 7 · The 0.29 mm Contact-Force Paradox

Four robots, one sticky drawer: pose tokens stall at 0%; sparse force tokens rescue all four.

In impedance control, contact force lives in p_cmd − p_meas.21,22 Replaying achieved poses erases spring deflection (Franka 83% → 14% at 0.29 mm error). A sparse deadband Newton channel active on just 30.8% of frames hits 81.7% 3-arm rescue—beating 100% dense force (80.0%) with zero free-space regression.

Act 05Exp 5 & Exp 8 · Unifying Contact Physics & Closed-Loop Adaptation

One physical law closes the loop: free-space residuals are drift; contact residuals are force.

Blind proprioceptive feedback rescues long chunks (k=32: 32% → 52%) yet degrades under contact drag. Freezing drift updates during contact (74.7% of chunks) lifts k=32 further to 56.7% at +0.07 drift (and 50.0% at brutal +0.10 drift) at 0 extra VLM tokens.3,4,5,23,24
